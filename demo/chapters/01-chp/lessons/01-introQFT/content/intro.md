## Introduction to Quantum Field Theory

Quantum Field Theory (QFT) is the theoretical framework that combines classical field theory, special relativity, and quantum mechanics. 

Instead of particles being the fundamental building blocks of the universe, QFT proposes that the universe is made of continuous, vibrating fluid-like substances known as **fields**. The vacuum of space is not truly empty; it is teeming with fluctuating fields. In QFT, what we perceive as particles—such as electrons and photons—are actually localized excitations (or "ripples") in these underlying quantum fields.

### Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)

Quantum Electrodynamics is the relativistic quantum field theory of electrodynamics. It describes how light and matter interact and is the first theory where full agreement between quantum mechanics and special relativity is achieved. QED mathematically describes all phenomena involving electrically charged particles interacting by means of exchange of photons, including the vacuum containing virtual photons that constantly pop in and out of existence.
