Charge is a mystery

Electric charge is one of the most fundamental properties of matter — and nobody truly knows why it exists.

We know charges come in two types: positive and negative. We know like charges repel and opposite charges attract. We know the electron carries a charge of exactly $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ coulombs.

But why does charge exist at all? Why are there exactly two types? Why is the electron's charge equal and opposite to the proton's, even though they are completely different particles?

These questions remain unanswered. Charge is simply a primitive fact about the universe.

What we do know

Charge is:

  • Quantized — it comes in discrete multiples of a base unit
  • Conserved — the total charge of an isolated system never changes
  • Lorentz invariant — charge doesn't change when you move fast
Simulation

In quantum field theory, charge isn't just a label — it emerges from a deep mathematical symmetry.

Specifically, electric charge is the conserved quantity associated with U(1) gauge symmetry. This is Noether's theorem in action: every continuous symmetry of a physical system corresponds to a conserved quantity.

The U(1) symmetry means we can rotate the phase of a quantum field — multiply it by $e^{i\theta}$ — and the physics doesn't change. The charge is what "counts" how the field transforms under this rotation.

Charge as we know it

In everyday life, charge shows up as static electricity, lightning, and the flow of current through wires.

The unit of charge is the coulomb (C). One coulomb is approximately $6.24 \times 10^{18}$ elementary charges — an enormous number.

Coulomb's law tells us the force between two point charges:

$$F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$

where $k \approx 8.99 \times 10^9$ N·m²/C².

⚡ Important

Conservation of charge is one of the most precisely tested laws in physics — verified to 1 part in 10²¹.

ℹ Note

Conservation of charge is one of the most precisely tested laws in all of physics.

In any isolated system, the total electric charge never changes. Charge can move, it can be transferred — but it cannot be created or destroyed.

This has been verified to better than 1 part in $10^{21}$.
